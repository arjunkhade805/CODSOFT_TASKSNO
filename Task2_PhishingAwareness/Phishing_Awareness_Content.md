# Phishing Awareness Guide

## What is Phishing?
Phishing is a cyberattack where attackers impersonate a trusted entity (bank, company, or person) to trick victims into revealing sensitive information such as passwords, credit card numbers, or personal data.

## How to Identify Suspicious Emails
- Sender address looks slightly off (e.g., "support@paypa1.com" instead of "support@paypal.com")
- Urgent or threatening language ("Your account will be suspended in 24 hours")
- Generic greetings like "Dear Customer" instead of your actual name
- Unexpected attachments or links
- Poor grammar and spelling mistakes
- Requests for sensitive info (passwords, OTPs, card numbers)

## How to Identify Fake Login Pages
- Check the URL carefully — look for misspellings or extra characters
- Look for HTTPS and a valid padlock icon (though attackers can fake this too)
- Legitimate sites never ask for your password via email link
- Hover over links before clicking to preview the real destination URL

## How to Identify Fraudulent Websites
- No proper "Contact Us" or "About" page
- Too-good-to-be-true offers or discounts
- Domain age is very recent (can check via WHOIS lookup)
- Poor website design or broken links

## Common Social Engineering Techniques
1. **Pretexting** – Attacker creates a fabricated scenario (e.g., pretending to be IT support) to gain trust.
2. **Baiting** – Offering something enticing (free downloads, prizes) to lure victims.
3. **Spear Phishing** – Highly targeted phishing aimed at a specific individual or organization using personal details.
4. **Vishing** – Voice phishing done over phone calls, pretending to be bank/support staff.
5. **Smishing** – Phishing via SMS messages with malicious links.

## Practical Security Tips
- Never click links in unsolicited emails/messages
- Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) wherever possible
- Verify sender identity through a separate channel before sharing info
- Keep software and browsers updated
- Use spam filters and antivirus/security tools
- Report phishing emails to your email provider or IT team

## Case Study Example
**Case**: In 2020, several Twitter employees were targeted through a phone spear-phishing attack. Attackers posed as IT staff and convinced employees to share internal credentials, leading to a major account takeover incident affecting high-profile accounts.

**Lesson**: Even employees trained in security can fall for well-crafted social engineering attacks — verification protocols and skepticism toward "urgent" requests are critical.

## Short Quiz

**Q1.** What should you check first if an email urgently asks you to "verify your account"?
- A) Ignore it
- B) Click the link immediately
- C) Check the sender's actual email address and hover over links
- D) Reply with your password

**Answer: C**

**Q2.** Which of these is a sign of a phishing website?
- A) HTTPS padlock present
- B) Slightly misspelled domain name
- C) Professional design
- D) Working contact page

**Answer: B**

**Q3.** What is "Vishing"?
- A) Phishing through video calls
- B) Phishing through voice/phone calls
- C) Phishing through vision-based apps
- D) A type of malware

**Answer: B**

## Conclusion
Phishing remains one of the most common and effective cyberattack methods because it exploits human trust rather than technical vulnerabilities. Awareness and skepticism toward unexpected or urgent requests are the strongest defenses against it.
